  • Kafka Outbox, the company-wide CDC and domain events platform (2022 - 2025). Publishing to Kafka straight from the applications meant dual writes, messages lost under load or network errors, different failure rates for the database and for Kafka, and no way to observe or guarantee SLIs such as freshness and delay along the pipeline. We designed and delivered a Change-Data-Capture solution based on the transactional outbox pattern: applications write to an outbox in the same transaction as their data, a MongoDB Kafka source connector attached to a replica reads the change stream, a transformer service (Kotlin / Spring Boot) normalises the records and publishes them to read-view topics, and a sweeper confirms delivery and cleans up. Only data that successfully committed to the global and multi-region databases reaches consumers, with delivery guaranteed end to end. A sibling solution covered AWS S3 publishing through its notification system and SQS.

    From there the solution grew into the company standard, and my part was mostly the adoption: I coordinated and executed the Ruby and Mongoid upgrades that let every application in the organisation take it, migrated every domain event published by the main monolith (some fifty event types) and by the satellite services to the outbox with the teams that owned them, and decommissioned the legacy publishers; once Kafka Connect's single message transforms could do the normalisation, we retired the transformer and removed a hop from the pipeline. When another team adopted PostgreSQL I made the outbox library ORM-agnostic (MongoDB and PostgreSQL/ActiveRecord, Mongoid 8 and 9, Rails 7 and 8) with TTL retention, dead-letter recovery runbooks and a sweeper covering both stores, extended the Kafka source connector to PostgreSQL, added change-time headers and rebuilt its build/configure/deploy pipeline with circuit-breaker rollbacks. I also looked after the alerting (freshness SLOs on the transformer, dead-letter queue alerts as Terraform on New Relic), the dashboards, the team's 2025 disaster recovery review and the multi-region gamedays for the streaming stack, and wrote an organisation-wide RuboCop cop that forbids creating database indexes in the foreground.

    Ruby, Mongoid, ActiveRecord, Kafka, Kafka Connect, Kotlin/Spring Boot, schema registry, Terraform, New Relic, OpenTelemetry, AWS S3 and SQS.

  • Quoteset persistence on S3 (2020 - 2021). The amount of data was approaching the hard limit of what the infrastructure could support. It saved the company a dramatic amount of money, made reads and writes faster and went through without any disruption.
